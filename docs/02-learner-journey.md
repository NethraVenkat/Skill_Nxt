# Learner Journey

## Journey map

| Stage | Learner action | Emotional goal | Drop-off risk | Prevention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First visit | Sees the live company preview and chooses “Start a venture” | “This is for someone like me.” | Cinematic visuals feel like a game with no learning | Show the six-step loop and a 60-second playable decision |
| Sign-up | Creates an account with email, Google, or magic link | Safe, low-friction commitment | Long form or unclear privacy | Ask only name, email, age confirmation, and consent; save progress immediately |
| Onboarding | Names venture, chooses idea or “I’m exploring,” sets weekly time | Ownership | Feels judged for having no idea | Provide three starter contexts and allow “exploring” without penalty |
| Skill check | Answers six scenario questions before theory | Curiosity and honest self-awareness | Treats it as an exam | Explain that it sets practice difficulty, not a rank; allow retry later |
| First lesson | Makes a decision before the insight, sees consequence, retries | Productive surprise: “My choice changed the company.” | Confusion about numbers | Narrate one variable at a time; offer a plain-English glossary |
| Module rhythm | Alternates simulator, lab, AI practice, and real mission | Momentum and relevance | Mission feels like homework | Give a 20-minute minimum path, examples of valid proof, and a save-and-return state |
| Module completion | Passes mastery test and sees skill map update | Competence | Fails and feels blocked | Unlock remediation cards and a second attempt; never erase work |
| Cohort room | Reviews peers in a group of 5–8 | Belonging and useful accountability | Fear of public criticism | Use sentence starters, blind first review, and explicit evidence-based rubrics |
| Midpoint | Replays early decisions with spaced events | Growth: “I now see what I missed.” | Novelty fades | Surface changed outcomes and one unresolved weak spot, not generic badges |
| All modules | Finishes passport sections and records pitch | Identity as a builder | Scope feels too large | Show “three decisions to Demo Day,” then break work into daily cards |
| Demo Day | Presents a 3–5 minute pitch and answers questions | Courage and recognition | Performance anxiety | Private rehearsal with AI Investor, optional recorded submission, clear rubric |
| After Demo Day | Reviews evidence, chooses next experiment | Agency beyond the course | Completion becomes an endpoint | Create a 30-day experiment plan and preserve the Venture Passport |

## Step-by-step experience

### 1. First visit

The landing screen opens on a living company panel: cash, customers, and runway move as a simulated decision is made. The call to action is **Run your first decision**, not “Read more.” The learner chooses between two pricing actions, sees the cash/customer trade-off, and receives the message: “You just practised venture judgement.”

The page also shows the six-step loop, the nine-module map, a privacy statement, and a realistic completion promise: “10 minutes to your first lesson; 16 weeks to pitch-ready at two sessions per week.”

### 2. Sign-up

The learner selects email magic link, Google, or GitHub. After authentication, the app creates a profile and an empty venture in one transaction. If the link expires, the error says what happened and offers a resend. No learning data is collected before consent.

### 3. Onboarding

The onboarding wizard has four screens:

1. **Name your venture:** name, one-sentence idea, and an “I’m exploring” option.
2. **Choose a context:** student life, local business, work productivity, health/wellbeing (non-clinical), climate/community, or custom.
3. **Set your learning contract:** two, three, or five sessions per week; preferred reminder day; cohort participation preference.
4. **Skill check:** six short scenarios covering problem clarity, customer evidence, pricing, MVP scope, teamwork, and pitching.

The wizard previews the initial virtual company: ₹10,00,000 cash, ₹83,333 monthly base burn, 12 months runway, 0 customers, 20 morale, and ₹0 valuation. The learner can edit only their idea and schedule; the starting state is fixed for fair comparison.

### 4. First lesson

Module 1, Lesson 1 begins with a customer complaint and three possible responses. The learner decides in 30 seconds. The simulator then changes customer count, morale, cash, and evidence quality. A short explainer names the concept, followed by a retry with a new customer. The learner earns mastery progress only after the retry and a real mission submission.

### 5. Completing the nine modules

Each module uses the same shell:

1. A 30-second challenge.
2. A decision and consequence.
3. A two-to-three-minute interactive insight.
4. A retry.
5. A real mission with proof.
6. A retrieval review scheduled later.

The Venture HQ shows the next best action, the current weak spot, and the next review due. A module remains “in progress” until its required mission proof is accepted and mastery test is passed.

### 6. Demo Day

After Module 9, the learner’s Passport must contain a problem statement, five or more customer evidence entries, MVP experiment result, pricing model, funnel metrics, team plan, funding ask, and scaling risk. The learner rehearses with AI Investor, submits a 3–5 minute pitch or presents live, receives two peer reviews, and sees a rubric-based result. Passing means “ready for the next experiment,” not “guaranteed startup success.”

## Accessibility and recovery

- Every lesson saves after each step and can resume at the last completed step.
- Keyboard navigation, visible focus, captions for any audio, reduced-motion mode, and screen-reader labels are required.
- A learner can pause a streak for one week without losing mastery.
- Offline-friendly drafts are stored locally and reconciled when online; proof uploads show explicit pending state.
- Every error has a recovery action, never a silent reset.
