# Curriculum Specification

## Progression rules

There are nine modules. Each has four lessons, one Concept Lab, one simulator challenge, one real-world mission, and one mastery test. A learner may view the next module’s overview, but lessons unlock only when the previous module’s mission is accepted and mastery score is at least 75/100. Remediation does not lower previous scores.

## Module 1 — Finding Problems

### Objectives

By the end, the learner can:

1. Write a problem statement with a specific person, context, frequency, and consequence.
2. Separate an observed pain from a solution idea in at least four of five examples.
3. Rank problems using frequency, severity, and current workaround scores.
4. Select one narrow problem worth validating.

### Lessons

1. **Notice the friction:** distinguish a complaint from a problem.
2. **Choose the person:** define a reachable first segment.
3. **Measure the pain:** score frequency, severity, and workaround.
4. **Frame the opportunity:** write and defend a problem statement.

### Concept Lab

**Customer Persona Builder** is used to turn a broad audience into a reachable first segment and expose unsupported assumptions.

### Simulator challenge

The company has three possible problems and ₹10,00,000 cash. Choosing a broad “everyone” problem creates high research cost and low conversion; choosing a narrow, frequent problem creates fewer initial prospects but stronger evidence.

### Real-world mission

Interview five people who may experience the problem, without pitching a solution. Submit anonymised notes, exact quotes, frequency counts, and a revised problem statement.

### Mastery test

Score at least 75/100: 30 points for a specific problem statement, 25 for separating observations from assumptions, 25 for evidence from five conversations, and 20 for a defensible ranking. At least four of five retrieval questions must be correct.

## Module 2 — Knowing Your Customer

### Objectives

By the end, the learner can:

1. Define a primary customer segment with inclusion and exclusion rules.
2. Map a customer’s current journey and identify a high-friction moment.
3. Conduct a neutral interview using open questions and reflective listening.
4. Convert evidence into three testable customer hypotheses.

### Lessons

1. **Segment, do not stereotype:** choose a reachable group.
2. **Map the moment:** locate trigger, workaround, and desired outcome.
3. **Ask and listen:** avoid leading questions.
4. **Turn evidence into hypotheses:** label confidence and next test.

### Concept Lab

**Customer Persona Builder** adds a journey map, evidence confidence, and assumption tags.

### Simulator challenge

The learner chooses between an expensive survey of 1,000 generic users, five targeted interviews, or shadowing two users. The correct choice depends on the unknown: attitudes require interviews; observed behaviour requires shadowing.

### Real-world mission

Complete five interviews or observations with the chosen segment. Submit consent confirmation, anonymised notes, three repeated patterns, one surprising contradiction, and three hypotheses with confidence levels.

### Mastery test

At least 75/100: 25 points for segment boundaries, 25 for a complete journey, 25 for neutral questions, 15 for evidence tagging, and 10 for a next test. No hypothesis may be presented as a fact.

## Module 3 — Validating the Idea

### Objectives

By the end, the learner can:

1. Select the riskiest assumption and state a falsifiable prediction.
2. Design a test with audience, behaviour, threshold, timebox, and interpretation.
3. Distinguish interest, intent, and payment evidence.
4. Decide whether to persevere, pivot, or stop based on results.

### Lessons

1. **Find the riskiest assumption:** prioritise what can kill the venture.
2. **Design a fair test:** define threshold before collecting data.
3. **Read signals:** separate compliments from commitment.
4. **Make the call:** use evidence to persevere, pivot, or stop.

### Concept Lab

**Funnel Leak Finder** models awareness, interest, activation, conversion, retention, and referral.

### Simulator challenge

The learner allocates ₹1,00,000 and two months among a landing page, prototype, paid pilot, and survey. Vanity sign-ups raise interest but not validated demand; paid pilots cost more but provide stronger evidence.

### Real-world mission

Run one timeboxed validation test with a pre-declared success threshold. Submit the test plan, timestamped evidence, results, and a decision memo that states what changed.

### Mastery test

At least 75/100: 25 points for the riskiest assumption, 25 for a falsifiable test, 25 for correct signal interpretation, 15 for threshold discipline, and 10 for an evidence-based decision. The learner must not change the threshold after seeing results.

## Module 4 — Building an MVP

### Objectives

1. Define one target outcome and exclude non-essential features.
2. Choose the lowest-cost experiment that can produce behavioural evidence.
3. Write acceptance criteria for a usable MVP.
4. Use a test result to prioritise the next build.

### Lessons

1. Outcome before feature.
2. Concierge, prototype, or product?
3. Scope the smallest useful test.
4. Learn from usage, not compliments.

### Concept Lab

**Business Model Canvas** with an MVP scope overlay.

### Simulator challenge

Choose a ₹3,00,000 full build, ₹60,000 concierge service, or ₹1,00,000 clickable prototype. Each produces different learning speed, customer capacity, and cash impact.

### Real-world mission

Deliver an MVP to three target users and record completion, time-to-value, and one failure point.

### Mastery test

75/100: one outcome, three acceptance criteria, a justified scope, observed usage from three users, and a prioritised next experiment.

## Module 5 — Pricing & Money

### Objectives

1. Calculate price, variable cost, contribution margin, and break-even customers.
2. Choose a price hypothesis tied to customer value and willingness to pay.
3. Explain cash flow versus profit in a simple monthly forecast.
4. Run a price test without misleading customers.

### Lessons

1. Value is not cost.
2. Contribution margin.
3. Break-even and runway.
4. Test the price.

### Concept Lab

**Pricing Lab** and **Break-even Machine**.

### Simulator challenge

Set price and service level for a product with ₹120 variable cost and ₹83,333 base burn. Low price increases conversion but extends break-even; high price reduces volume but protects runway.

### Real-world mission

Present two price options to five target customers and secure at least one paid or refundable commitment where appropriate.

### Mastery test

75/100: correct calculations, a value-based rationale, a cash forecast, and evidence from a price conversation or transaction.

## Module 6 — Marketing & Growth

### Objectives

1. Select one channel based on customer behaviour and economics.
2. Calculate funnel conversion and identify the largest leak.
3. Write a message that names a problem and outcome without hype.
4. Design a small growth experiment with a stop rule.

### Lessons

1. Where customers already look.
2. Message-market fit.
3. Find the funnel leak.
4. Growth with a stop rule.

### Concept Lab

**Funnel Leak Finder** with cohort comparison.

### Simulator challenge

Allocate ₹50,000 among Instagram, WhatsApp referrals, campus ambassadors, and local partnerships. Each has a different reach, conversion, trust, and learning speed.

### Real-world mission

Run one channel experiment for seven days and submit source-tagged traffic, conversions, cost, and one message revision.

### Mastery test

75/100: correct funnel math, channel rationale, clear message, and a stop/continue decision supported by results.

## Module 7 — Team & Leadership

### Objectives

1. Define roles by outcomes and decision rights.
2. Give feedback using observation, impact, and next step.
3. Recognise morale, workload, and conflict signals.
4. Make a fair hiring or partnership trade-off.

### Lessons

1. Roles and decision rights.
2. Hiring for the bottleneck.
3. Feedback without blame.
4. Protecting team energy.

### Concept Lab

**Team Capacity Map** (a Business Model Canvas companion).

### Simulator challenge

Choose a co-founder, contractor, or no hire for a product bottleneck. Each changes burn, speed, quality, morale, and founder time.

### Real-world mission

Run a 20-minute role/feedback conversation with a collaborator or peer and submit an anonymised action agreement.

### Mastery test

75/100: clear decision rights, justified resource choice, a feedback script, and a realistic capacity plan.

## Module 8 — Funding & Pitching

### Objectives

1. Tell a problem-evidence-solution story in three minutes.
2. Build a simple use-of-funds plan and milestone.
3. Answer a tough question with evidence and uncertainty.
4. Choose funding only when its cost and control trade-offs fit the venture.

### Lessons

1. The evidence-led pitch.
2. The ask and milestone.
3. Tough questions.
4. Funding choices and trade-offs.

### Concept Lab

**Pitch Evidence Mapper** inside Venture Passport.

### Simulator challenge

Choose bootstrapping, a grant, a loan, or equity. The choice changes cash, control, repayment risk, and milestone pressure.

### Real-world mission

Record a three-minute pitch to a real listener, collect two questions, and revise one slide or claim.

### Mastery test

75/100: 25 points problem/evidence, 25 solution and economics, 20 ask/use, 20 answers, and 10 evidence integrity.

## Module 9 — Scaling

### Objectives

1. Identify the constraint that will limit the next growth step.
2. Model quality, cash, and team effects of scaling.
3. Choose a repeatable process and leading indicator.
4. Write a 30-day scale experiment with a rollback plan.

### Lessons

1. Find the constraint.
2. Repeatable delivery.
3. Scale quality, not chaos.
4. The next 30 days.

### Concept Lab

**Business Model Canvas** with constraint and operating-system overlays.

### Simulator challenge

The learner doubles demand after a viral event and chooses to scale operations, pause marketing, or accept a backlog. Customer satisfaction, cash, and morale respond differently.

### Real-world mission

Document one repeatable process, run it twice, measure the leading indicator, and write a rollback plan.

### Mastery test

75/100: identifies the constraint, models one trade-off, documents a repeatable process, and sets a measurable 30-day experiment.
