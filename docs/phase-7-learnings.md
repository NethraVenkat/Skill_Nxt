# Phase 7 learning review: where community features should target drop-off

## Source of analytics used

This repository does not yet have a live /admin/learning dataset with real cohort or mission activity. The findings below are therefore based on the product's seeded assumptions, the existing Phase 5 mission flows, the Phase 6 course design, and the known drop-off patterns typical of beginner founder learning platforms.

## Likely solo-learner drop-off points

### 1. Before the first real mission

Learners often do not complete the first real mission because they feel uncertain, unprepared, or embarrassed. This is the most likely place to add community support without introducing performance pressure.

Target feature:
- a gentle “You are not alone” onboarding screen
- cohort invitation before the first mission
- a buddy pair or starter-circle prompt

### 2. After a weak or confusing AI feedback report

If a learner sees AI feedback that feels impersonal or too harsh, they often abandon the mission or the module.

Target feature:
- structured peer review calibration before real review work
- “what to improve next” thread with a clear, specific suggestion
- optional mentor review on first flagged submission

### 3. During the middle of the curriculum

The biggest risk is not that learners fail a skill; it is that they stop returning after a few weeks. This is especially common around Modules 5–7, where the app mixes money, growth, and team realities.

Target feature:
- weekly goal ritual
- buddy nudges
- a “stuck on this concept?” module thread

### 4. Before Demo Day or final capstone

Learners often stall at the end because they feel “not ready” or fear public judgment.

Target feature:
- rehearsal circles
- readiness score from feedback and AI investor practice
- milestone-based Demo Day qualification rather than a surprise final push

## Missions most likely to be abandoned

Based on the mission structure and likely beginner behavior, the most abandoned missions are likely to be:

1. pricing and money missions
2. growth experiments with measurable numbers
3. team/leadership or hiring conversations
4. any mission that requires multiple interviews or a recorded proof artifact
5. capstone preparation and pitch rehearsal

Why they are abandoned:
- they feel more vulnerable than earlier research tasks
- they require more effort and more personal accountability
- they are perceived as “public” or “judged” rather than just learning tasks

Community fixes:
- buddy review before submission
- lower-friction safer alternatives
- short weekly accountability rituals
- structured review assignments so learners know what “good” looks like

## When learners stop returning

Most likely return gaps:

- 3–5 days after a missed goal or a confusing score
- 2–3 days after a mission revision request with vague feedback
- after a missed weekly check-in or a large gap in cohort activity

What should be targeted:
- not just reminders, but a tiny social ritual
- a clear next action
- an “ask help” flow that invites conversation without shaming

## Cohort features matched to drop-off points

### For early drop-off (pre-mission)
- starter circle or buddy pair
- “what is your weekly goal?” ritual
- short first-session kickoff template

### For confusion and abandonment after feedback
- calibration review
- reviewer quality score
- mentor review access for submission disputes

### For mid-course non-return
- weekly check-ins
- buddy nudges
- module threads and a visible progress bar

### For end-of-course stalls
- Demo Day rehearsal groups
- readiness score and peer pitch practice
- shared cohort achievements and recognition

## Design rules we should apply in Phase 7

1. Small groups matter more than big groups.
2. Structure beats open chat.
3. Peer review must be trained, not improv.
4. Recognition should celebrate effort and helpfulness, not just top valuation.
5. Under-18 learners need stricter privacy and safer spaces.
6. Cohorts should help each learner act, not simply watch.

## Approval gate

These are the major design observations before building. The actual implementation should begin only after the Phase 7 plan is reviewed and approved.
